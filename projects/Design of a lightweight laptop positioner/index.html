Design of a lightweight laptop positioner

Topology-optimized laptop positioner for improved ergonomics.

Fusion (CAD) Abaqus (FEA) HyperMesh + OptiStruct + HyperView (Topology Optimization) Python (calculations and post-processing) MATLAB (mechanism design)

1. Problem statement and Goals

I originally used a custom-made raised platform on my desk (see Figure 1) to elevate my laptop. This setup allowed me to use an iPad alongside the laptop for note-taking, or pair the laptop with an external keyboard and mouse to reduce eye-level strain.

However, when using the laptop on its own, several critical ergonomic issues emerged:

  • Poor visibility due to distance: The screen was too far away, forcing the user to lean forward and adopt poor posture.
  • Insufficient height: The display remained below eye level, causing severe neck tilt and slouching.
  • Inefficient workflow: Manually moving the laptop off the platform onto the desk improved readability slightly, but degraded ergonomics even further while being tedious and inefficient.

Goals to achieve are:

  • Design a mechanism responsible for adjusting the laptop’s position to improve ergonomics.
  • Measure/estimate loads and apply appropriate factor of safety.
  • Perform topology optimization of the 3D printed parts for a material-efficient design.
  • Conduct approximate fatigue calculations to estimate assembly lifespan.

2. Design

  • Based on the two known poses of the laptop (on the fixed platform & close to the user) and its orientation between these two (parallel to the desk surface), an articulated parallelogram mechanism was selected to solve this problem.
  • Link lengths were iteratively optimized in MATLAB by solving kinematics for different configurations, considering platform dimensions, ergonomic target pose, and desk footprint constraints.
  • Weight distribution of the laptop was measured, while the one of the mobile platform as well as the accidental lateral force were estimated.
  • Initial simple topologies for the extension arms, support links, and mobile platform were modeled in Fusion 360.
  • The geometry was imported into HyperMesh to define design spaces and apply specific manufacturing constraints for topology optimization:
    • Extrusion constraint: Applied to the mobile plank to ensure simple manufacturability (cutting out material).
    • Overhang angle constraint: Applied to the extension arms and support links to optimize for 3D printing.
